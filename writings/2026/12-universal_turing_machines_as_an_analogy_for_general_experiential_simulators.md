# universal turing machines as analogy for general experiential simulators

(just an analogy)

for those rusty on the concept of turing machines, the key idea is that they are used to model (inter-subjective) human thinking, and that universal turing machines are general emulators for other turing machines. the turing machine is a mathematical construct. a concept. they are not a real thing, but useful here as an analogy.

it is well known that experiences can be simulated. the evidence is that we are the experiential analogy of a universal turing machine (autm). **as autm we can enter simulations and perceive them as “real” experiences**, as is undeniably proven feasible by modern computer technology.

what is less appreciated, and what completes the argument for the simulation thesis, is that as autm we can “jump out of” a simulation by entering into another simulation that while having a different “reality”, also has the existing world as a simulation, and we are currently immersed in the simulation inside it.

![image](images/simulating_worlds.jpg)

while it may seem like this doesn’t work “computationally” because the layers of simulation gets deeper and deeper, it would become obvious to anyone accepting this mode of thinking that “base reality” does not exist and it’s turtles all the way down. also, do note that we are talking about utms as analogy (hence “autm”), not as a real thing. computational power has no meaning here, since base reality is not the current world our human bodies are residing in -- the autm runs its own simulation, not our bodies, not computers, and not within perceived-time.

the interesting thing about “jumping out” of a simulation by **conceptually** constructing a new simulation is that you do not actually have to experientially do it. you can just “know” that your current experiences are part of a simulation, and that the “program” your autm is running has extra bits that describe the “outer world” in addition to your direct experiences.

“jumping out” is the same as “jumping in”, and because the subjective experience is identical after jumping in, it seems quite valid to posit that we are in the “already jumped in” state. in other words, because you can imagine this being possible, it seems valid to posit it to be true. argument appears to be “ontological” (unfortunately), but i suppose it’s of the good kind.

this is only a crude analogy. we haven’t yet dealt with the issue of simulation leakage (with less-than-perfect simulations), or how the actual simulation works mechanically, or how the different autms work together despite the leakages (divergent leakages observed empirically). but there is one extra corollary: the autm structure is functionally identical. and thus while there is no “base reality”, the “simulation as a structure of experiential existence” implies that all autms, and thus all experiential subjects, are equal.
